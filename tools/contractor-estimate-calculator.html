Contractor job estimate calculator

This calculator helps contractors, remodelers, installers and small service businesses build a quick project-price estimate from the main cost drivers: materials, labor, overhead and markup.

How the estimate is calculated

Labor cost = Labor hours × Hourly labor rate
Direct cost = Materials + Labor cost
Overhead = Direct cost × Overhead %
Cost base = Direct cost + Overhead
Estimated price = Cost base × (1 + Markup %)

Example

With $5,000 of materials, 80 labor hours at $45/hour, 12% overhead and 20% markup, direct cost is $8,600, overhead is $1,032 and the estimated project price is about $11,558.40.

What should overhead include?

Depending on the business, overhead can include vehicles, insurance, office costs, non-billable supervision, software, small tools and other operating expenses. Keep your method consistent so estimates are comparable.

Country and local-cost note

The math works with USD, GBP, CAD, AUD and EUR, but local labor rates, permits, taxes, insurance requirements and material prices vary widely. Treat the result as a pricing worksheet rather than a substitute for a detailed scope of work.

Common questions

Is markup the same as margin?

No. Markup is calculated on cost, while profit margin is profit divided by selling price. A 20% markup does not equal a 20% margin.

Should contingency be included?

For uncertain work, many estimators track contingency separately rather than hiding it inside labor or material cost.
