Freelance hourly rate calculator

A freelancer's billable rate has to cover more than personal income. Business expenses, non-billable time and a reserve for taxes or other obligations can materially change the rate needed to reach an annual target.

How the calculator works

Required annual revenue = (Desired income + Business expenses) ÷ (1 − Reserve %)
Billable hours per year = Working weeks × Billable hours per week
Hourly rate = Required annual revenue ÷ Billable hours per year

Example

With an $80,000 personal-income target, $15,000 of annual business expenses and a 25% reserve, modeled required revenue is about $126,666.67. At 46 working weeks and 25 billable hours per week, that is 1,150 billable hours and a modeled hourly rate of about $110.14.

Why billable hours matter

Administrative work, sales, proposals, learning, bookkeeping and time between projects are often not billable. Using total working hours instead of realistic billable hours can understate the rate needed.

Common questions

Is the reserve percentage a tax calculation?

No. It is only a planning reserve. Tax treatment depends on your country, structure and personal circumstances.

Should I charge exactly the calculated rate?

Not necessarily. Market positioning, project risk, value, demand, scope and minimum project size can all affect pricing.
